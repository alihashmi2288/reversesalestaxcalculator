## H2: How New York Sales Tax Works 

New York sales tax has three parts: 

- **State base rate:** 4% applies statewide. 

- **Local rate:** Counties and cities add their own rates. Local rates range from 0% to 4.875%. 

- **Special district rate:** The Metropolitan Commuter Transportation District (MCTD) adds 0.375% in certain downstate jurisdictions. 

The combined rate is the state rate plus the local rate plus any special district rate. Combined rates in New York range from 4% to 8.875%. 

The rate you pay depends on where the taxable item or service is delivered. It does not depend on where you live. This is the destination rule. It applies to most taxable purchases in New York.

## H2: Reverse Sales Tax Calculation 

The reverse calculation formula is: 

- **Pre-tax price = Total price ÷ (1 + Combined rate)** 

- **Tax amount = Total price – Pre-tax price** 

**Example:** You paid $217.75 for an item in New York City. You want to find the pre-tax price. 

1. Combined rate: 8.875% (0.08875 as a decimal) 

2. Divisor: 1 + 0.08875 = 1.08875 

3. Pre-tax price: $217.75 ÷ 1.08875 = $200.00 

4. Tax amount: $217.75 – $200.00 = $17.75 

The pre-tax price is $200.00. The tax is $17.75.

## H2: What Is Exempt from New York Sales Tax? 

New York exempts certain items from sales tax. These exemptions apply at the state level. Some local jurisdictions also honor them. 

**Groceries:** Most food sold for home consumption is exempt. This includes unprepared food items sold in grocery stores. Restaurant meals, prepared food, candy, soft drinks, and alcoholic beverages are taxable. 

**Clothing and footwear:** Items priced under $110 per item or pair are exempt from the 4% state rate and from local rates in jurisdictions that adopted the exemption. Items priced at $110 or more are fully taxable. Some counties tax clothing regardless of price. Verify the rule for your specific location. 

**Prescription and nonprescription medicines:** Exempt from sales tax. 

**Prepared food:** Taxable at the full combined rate. This includes hot food, food sold for immediate consumption, and most restaurant meals. 

For a complete list of exemptions, see the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance quick reference guide.
