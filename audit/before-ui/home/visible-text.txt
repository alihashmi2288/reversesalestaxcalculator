A reverse sales tax calculator works backwards from a tax-included total to find the original price before tax was added. Most calculators start with a base price and add tax on top. The reverse sales tax formula divides rather than subtracts, which prevents significant accounting discrepancies over time. This tool fixes that problem instantly. The term reverse tax refers to working backwards from a final amount to isolate the base price and the tax component separately. Math Logic The Reverse Sales Tax Formula The mathematical formula is straightforward: Figure 1: Reverse sales tax formula breakdown ($107.50 receipt total divided by 1.075 equals $100.00 base pre-tax amount). The Standard Equation Original Price = Final Price ÷ (1 + Tax Rate / 100) That is the sales tax reverse formula used by accountants, standard accounting practice, and financial reporting tools worldwide. How the math breaks down step-by-step: Take the tax rate and divide it by 100. So 8.5% becomes 0.085 . Add 1 to that number. You get 1.085 . Divide the total price you paid by 1.085 . The result is the original pre-tax price. Subtract the original price from the total to find the exact tax amount paid. Worked Example: You paid $215.70 . The tax rate is 8.25% . 1. RATE DECIMAL 8.25 ÷ 100 = 0.0825 1 + 0.0825 = 1.0825 2. ORIGINAL PRICE $215.70 ÷ 1.0825 = $199.26 3. TAX AMOUNT $215.70 − $199.26 = $16.44 paid Many people try to figure sales tax from total amounts by multiplying the total by the tax rate. That produces the wrong answer because the tax rate applies to the base price, not the total. Division is the only correct method. Our calculator applies this formula automatically every time. Knowledge Base Reverse Sales Tax Frequently Asked Questions Everything you need to know about calculating pre-tax price, statutory sales tax rates, and business accounting rules. Basics How It Works What is a reverse sales tax calculator? A reverse sales tax calculator works backwards from the total price you paid (including tax) to find the original pre-tax price. Instead of adding tax onto a price, it removes the tax to reveal what the item actually cost before tax was applied. This is the exact opposite of a standard sales tax calculator. What is the formula for reverse sales tax? The formula is: Original Price = Final Price ÷ (1 + Tax Rate / 100). For example, if you paid $107.50 and the tax rate is 7.5%, the calculation is: $107.50 ÷ 1.075 = $100.00. The tax amount paid was $7.50. Why do you divide instead of just subtracting the tax percentage? Because sales tax is applied to the pre-tax price, not the final total. If you incorrectly subtract 7.5% from $107.50, you get $107.50 × 0.925 = $99.44 (wrong). The correct method divides: $107.50 ÷ 1.075 = $100.00, then $100.00 × 0.075 = $7.50 tax. Division is the mathematical inverse of multiplication, which is what the original tax calculation performed. How do I calculate the original price before tax? Step 1: Convert your tax rate to a decimal by dividing by 100 (e.g., 8.5% → 0.085). Step 2: Add 1 to that decimal (1 + 0.085 = 1.085). Step 3: Divide your total price by that number ($115.13 ÷ 1.085 = $106.11 pre-tax price). Or just use our calculator — it does all three steps instantly. What is the backwards tax calculator and is it the same thing? Yes. A backwards tax calculator, backwards sales tax calculator, and back out tax calculator are all the same tool described with different search terms. They all reverse calculate from a tax-inclusive total to find the original price. What is the difference between sales tax and VAT? Sales tax in the US is collected only at the final point of sale and paid only by the end consumer. VAT (Value Added Tax), used in Europe and many other countries, is collected at each stage of the supply chain — from manufacturer to wholesaler to retailer. Tennessee has the highest combined average sales tax rate in the country at 9.60%, followed closely by Louisiana (9.50%), Arkansas (9.49%), Washington (9.40%), and Alabama (9.33%). A combined sales tax rate adds the state-level rate and any local (city, county, district) rates together. Is shipping taxable in the US? This varies by state. Some states (like California) generally exempt separately stated shipping charges. Others (like Texas) tax shipping when it is part of a taxable sale. The safest rule: if the underlying goods are taxable and shipping is bundled with the sale price, assume it is taxable in most states. What if I do not know whether shipping was taxed? Generally, US sales taxes apply to the sale of tangible personal property, not services. However, this varies significantly. Hawaii and New Mexico tax almost all services. Canada (GST, HST, PST, QST) What is the difference between GST, HST, PST, and QST in Canada? GST (Goods and Services Tax) is the federal Canadian tax at 5%, applicable everywhere. HST (Harmonized Sales Tax) combines the federal GST and provincial tax into a single rate, used in Ontario (13%), New Brunswick (15%), Nova Scotia (15%), Newfoundland (15%), and Prince Edward Island (15%). PST (Provincial Sales Tax) is a separate provincial tax charged alongside GST in British Columbia (7%), Saskatchewan (6%), and Manitoba (7%). QST (Quebec Sales Tax) is Quebec s unique provincial tax at 9.975%, applied alongside the federal GST. How do I calculate the price before HST in Ontario? Ontario s HST rate is 13%. Divide the final total by 1.13. Example: $226.00 ÷ 1.13 = $200.00 pre-tax price. The HST amount paid was $26.00. How do I remove GST from a price in Alberta? Alberta only charges the federal 5% GST with no provincial tax. Divide your total by 1.05. Example: $105.00 ÷ 1.05 = $100.00 pre-tax price. Can I use this calculator for Canadian QST calculations? Math Technical Questions What is the mathematical proof that division is correct for reverse tax? Forward tax: Post-tax = Pre-tax × (1 + rate/100). To reverse this algebraically: Pre-tax = Post-tax ÷ (1 + rate/100). This is basic algebra — dividing both sides by (1 + rate/100). There is no rounding or approximation involved; division is the exact mathematical inverse of the forward multiplication. What happens when I have a 0% tax rate? Entering 0% as the tax rate returns the original price equal to the total paid. No tax was collected, so there is nothing to extract. This is the correct result for transactions in tax-exempt states or on tax-exempt purchases. How do I handle a transaction where the tax amount on the receipt does not match what the reverse formula calculates? Small differences of one or two cents are normal. They result from how the merchant rounds individual item taxes before summing them. For accounting purposes, use the amounts printed on the receipt as your definitive record. Larger discrepancies suggest either a rate error or a mathematical error in the original transaction. Does the order of rounding matter in tax calculations? Yes, it can matter by a few cents on large transactions. Tax agencies specify rounding rules (e.g., round to the nearest cent after the final calculation, not after each step). Our calculator applies rounding only at the final display stage to maintain maximum precision throughout the computation. What if I have multiple tax rates applied (state + city + county)? Add all applicable rates together first, then divide by (1 + combined rate/100). For example, if state = 6%, county = 1%, city = 0.5%, the combined rate is 7.5%. Divide your total by 1.075. This works because all rates are applied to the same pre-tax base price simultaneously. Can I calculate sales tax if I only know the tax amount and the total? Yes.
