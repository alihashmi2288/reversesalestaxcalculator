Unlike the US state sales tax system (which is collected only at the final point of sale), Canada operates on a Value Added Tax (VAT) model. The base federal tax is the GST (Goods and Services Tax) at 5%. Most provinces combine this with a provincial tax to create the HST (Harmonized Sales Tax) at 13% to 15%. Other provinces charge GST and a separate PST (Provincial Sales Tax), while Quebec charges GST and QST (Quebec Sales Tax). If you re a business owner or consumer trying to extract the exact pre-tax cost from a receipt, you cannot simply subtract the tax percentage from the final total. CRA Input Tax Credit (ITC) Reverse Tax Fractions Under Subsection 169(1) of the Excise Tax Act (ETA) , GST/HST-registered Canadian businesses are entitled to recover the tax paid on commercial inputs by claiming an Input Tax Credit (ITC). When a supplier invoice or receipt displays only a tax-inclusive total, the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) authorizes the use of statutory ITC calculation fractions : 5% GST (Alberta, Territories) Total × 5 / 105 On a $105 receipt, $105 × (5/105) = $5.00 ITC. Pre-tax base is $100.00. 13% HST (Ontario) Total × 13 / 113 On a $565 receipt, $565 × (13/113) = $65.00 ITC. Pre-tax base is $500.00. 15% HST (Atlantic Canada) Total × 15 / 115 On a $230 receipt, $230 × (15/115) = $30.00 ITC. Pre-tax base is $200.00. Important Notice for BC, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba: In provinces with separate Provincial Sales Tax (PST/RST), only the federal 5% GST is claimable as an ITC . The provincial sales tax portion (7% in BC, 6% in SK, 7% in MB) is a non-recoverable retail sales tax that must be expensed in operating overhead rather than claimed on your CRA GST/HST return. Are groceries taxed in Canada? + Basic groceries (like milk, bread, and fresh vegetables) are generally zero-rated in Canada , meaning they are taxed at 0%. However, snack foods, hot prepared meals, and alcohol are fully taxable under standard GST/HST/PST/QST rules. Does the calculator work for Quebec s QST? + Yes! Why do Alberta and the Territories only charge 5%? + Alberta, the Northwest Territories, Nunavut, and Yukon do not have a provincial sales tax (PST). Residents and businesses in these areas only pay the federal 5% Goods and Services Tax (GST) on taxable items.
